## 1. Primary Objective

Redesign and implement a significantly elevated, modern version of the Schoolnet India website.

Position Schoolnet as:

> **A trusted technology and transformation partner for future-ready schools, while creating learning experiences students genuinely want to use.**

The redesigned experience must communicate:

1. **Credibility for school leaders and decision-makers**
2. **Innovation and technology leadership**
3. **Student excitement and engagement**
4. **Teacher empowerment**
5. **Proven impact at scale**
6. **Simple paths to demo, partnership, adoption, and login**

The **primary commercial audience is schools and school leadership**.

Students and teachers are important secondary audiences and should have dedicated, engaging journeys.

## 4. Core Recommendation — Make the Product Experience Sell the Partnership

Do not stop at marketing pages.

A major recommendation is to create a **show-don't-tell conversion journey**:

> **Homepage → Schools → Student Experience → Student Demo → Book a School Demo**

The website should allow a principal, school owner, or decision-maker to see what a student would actually experience before asking them to book a demo.

This is strategically important.

Instead of only claiming that Schoolnet makes learning engaging, the prototype should visually demonstrate it.

### Recommended journey

1. Visitor lands on the homepage.
2. Visitor sees scale, credibility, and school transformation proposition.
3. Visitor opens **Schools**.
4. The Schools page includes a section:
   **"See what your students will experience."**
5. Clicking it opens the Student Experience page.
6. Visitor can enter a **mock student demo** without creating a real account.
7. Student demo shows learning, progress, AI assistance, quizzes, recommendations, and achievements.
8. Persistent CTA:
   **"Bring this experience to your school → Book a Demo"**
9. CTA opens a school-focused demo request form.

The intended reaction from a principal should be:

> **"I can actually see what my students would get. I want to talk to Schoolnet."**

This journey should be treated as a **core conversion mechanism**, not an optional extra.

Also provide equivalent preview links for:

- **See the Teacher Experience**
- **See the School Admin Experience**

The student experience should remain the most visually engaging of the three.

## 29. Content Research Rules

Before implementation, research Schoolnet using publicly available web information.

Priority order:

1. **Official Schoolnet website**
2. **Official Schoolnet blogs, reports, and press releases**
3. **Official pages of named partners or government organizations**
4. **Credible media sources**

For each factual metric, internally maintain metadata such as:

```ts
{
  value: "100K+",
  label: "Schools",
  source: "Schoolnet official website",
  verified: true,
  lastChecked: "YYYY-MM-DD",
  placeholder: false
}
```

For unavailable information:

```ts
{
  value: "XX+",
  label: "Schools Transformed",
  source: null,
  verified: false,
  lastChecked: "YYYY-MM-DD",
  placeholder: true
}
```

Never convert a placeholder into a plausible-looking factual statistic.

Unacceptable unless sourced:

- `87% improvement`
- `14,562 schools`
- `92% student satisfaction`

Use instead:

- `XX%`
- `XX+`
- `To be confirmed`

## 31. Handling Conflicting Numbers

Schoolnet pages may contain figures representing different scopes or time periods.

Examples:

- smart classrooms
- KYAN installations
- schools served
- LMS schools
- students impacted
- teachers trained

Do not assume they are interchangeable.

When multiple figures exist:

1. Identify scope.
2. Identify source.
3. Identify publication/update date if available.
4. Prefer the newest clearly scoped figure.
5. Preserve wording explaining what is being counted.
6. If still ambiguous, use a placeholder or omit the number.

Accuracy is more important than filling every card.

## 49. Final Product Direction

Do not simply make the current Schoolnet site prettier.

Reframe the entire digital experience around:

## Schools want transformation.
## Teachers want technology that helps.
## Students want learning that engages them.

Schoolnet should visually and verbally connect those three needs.

The final experience should make a school principal think:

> **"This is a serious education transformation partner. I want to see a demo."**

A student should think:

> **"Learning through this looks much more interesting."**

A teacher should think:

> **"This could actually make teaching easier and more effective."**

And the prototype should prove those ideas through interactive experiences rather than relying only on marketing copy.
